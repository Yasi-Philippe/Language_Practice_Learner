# Adjectives that follow orders

> Like in Spanish, Italian adjectives agree with their noun. The only question is which family the adjective belongs to.

## La regola

| family | m. sing. | f. sing. | m. plur. | f. plur. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -o / -a | ross**o** | ross**a** | ross**i** | ross**e** |
| -e | verd**e** | verd**e** | verd**i** | verd**i** |
| invariable | **blu** | **blu** | **blu** | **blu** |

Invariable too: **rosa, viola, beige** — colors named after things or borrowed from other languages.

## Come in spagnolo

- Agreement in gender and number: *casas blancas* → **case bianche**.
- A mixed group goes masculine: *Paolo y Giulia son italianos* → **sono italiani**.
- Position can change the meaning, in both languages: **un grande uomo** (a great man) vs **un uomo grande** (a big man) — exactly *un gran hombre* vs *un hombre grande*. Same with **povero**: **un povero ragazzo** (poor thing) vs **un ragazzo povero** (no money).

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- *-e* adjectives make the plural with **-i**: *verdes* → **verdi**, *interesantes* → **interessanti**.
- **blu** never changes: **i pantaloni blu** — Spanish says *azules*.
- The hard sound gets an **h**: **bianco → bianchi / bianche**, **lungo → lunghi / lunghe**.

## Occhio!

- **molto, poco, troppo, tanto** agree when they go with a noun: **molti amici, poca gente, troppa pasta**. Before an adjective they don't change: **molto belle** (very beautiful). Spanish does the same with *muchos / muy* — the trap is that Italian uses one word, **molto**, for both.
- **simpatico → simpatici** (with **-ci**), but **simpatiche** in the feminine.

## Curiosità

Italian has two basic blues. **Blu** is dark blue, **azzurro** is sky blue — and Italians feel them as two different colors, the way you feel *rojo* and *rosa*. The national teams are **gli Azzurri**, after the blue of the House of Savoy, Italy's old royal family.

## Il trucco

Ask three questions. Ends in **-o**? Four forms. Ends in **-e**? Two forms (**-e / -i**). A color named after a thing (**rosa**, **viola**) or a borrowed word (**blu**, **beige**)? One form.
