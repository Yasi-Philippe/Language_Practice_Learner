# This, that — and the one Italy forgot

> **questo** is easy. **quello** behaves exactly like the definite article — so if you know **il, lo, l'**, you already know it.

## La regola

| article | il | lo | l' | la | i | gli | le |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **quello** | quel | quello | quell' | quella | quei | quegli | quelle |

**questo** simply agrees: **questo, questa, questi, queste** (and **quest'** before a vowel).

## Come in spagnolo

- **questo** ~ *este*, **quello** ~ *ese / aquel*. They agree like any adjective.
- They work as pronouns too: **voglio quello** = *quiero ese*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- Two distances instead of three: **quello** covers both *ese* and *aquel*.
- **quello** changes with the next word, like the article: **quel ragazzo, quello studente, quell'albero, quegli amici**.
- There's no neuter *esto / eso*: Italian uses **questo, quello**, or the formal **ciò**: **ciò che dici** = *lo que dices*.

## Occhio!

- **quei ragazzi** but **quegli zaini** — follow **i / gli** exactly.
- In speech **questo** shrinks to **'sto**: **'sto libro**. It's welded into everyday words: **stasera** (*questa sera*), **stamattina**, **stanotte**, **stavolta**.

## Curiosità

Italian once had a third demonstrative, just like Spanish *ese*: **codesto**, "that one near you". It's still alive in Tuscany and in bureaucratic letters — **codesto ufficio**, "your office" — but the rest of Italy let it go.

## Il trucco

Say the article in your head and swap in its **quel-** twin: **il → quel**, **lo → quello**, **i → quei**, **gli → quegli**, **le → quelle**.
