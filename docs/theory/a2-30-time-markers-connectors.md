# The glue words

> Little words that make you sound fluent — and a few friends that look Spanish but aren't.

## La regola

- **già** = *ya* · **non… ancora** = *todavía no* · **non… più** = *ya no*
- **appena** = *acabar de* (**sono appena arrivato**) or *en cuanto* (**appena arrivo**)
- **mai** = *nunca*, and *alguna vez* in questions · **sempre** = *siempre*
- **mentre** = *mientras* · **poi** = *luego* · **fa** = *hace* (**due giorni fa**)
- **quindi, perciò** = *así que* · **però, ma** = *pero* · **invece** = *en cambio* · **anche** = *también* · **anche se** = *aunque* · **allora** = *entonces, pues*

## Come in spagnolo

- **fa** and *hace* both mean "it makes": **due giorni fa** = *hace dos días* — from **fare** and *hacer*.
- **però** and *pero* are the same word, from Latin *per hoc*, "because of this". **perciò** ("therefore") is built the same way: **per** + **ciò**.
- **mentre** = *mientras*, **sempre** = *siempre*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- **fa** goes after the time: **un anno fa** (*hace un año*).
- Spanish *acabo de llegar* uses a verb; Italian uses an adverb: **sono appena arrivato**.
- **mai** also means "ever": **sei mai stato a Roma?** = *¿has estado alguna vez en Roma?*

## Occhio!

- **appena** isn't *apenas*: **sono appena arrivato** = *acabo de llegar*, not "I barely arrived". (It *can* mean "barely", but rarely.)
- **ancora** also means "again / more": **ancora un caffè?** = *¿otro café?*

## Curiosità

Italians love a little word Spanish doesn't have: **mica**. **Non è mica vero!** ≈ *¡Que no es verdad!* It comes from Latin *mica*, "crumb" — "not even a crumb" — the same word as Spanish *miga*. French did the same with *pas*, "a step": "not even a step".

## Il trucco

Spanish *hace* + time → time + **fa**. Spanish *acabar de* → **appena** + passato prossimo.
