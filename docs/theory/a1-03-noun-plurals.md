# Plurals without an "s"

> Spanish sticks an *-s* on the end: *libro → libros*. Italian never does — it swaps the last vowel instead: **libro → libri**.

## La regola

| singular | plural | example |
|---|---|---|
| **-o** | **-i** | il libr**o** → i libr**i** |
| **-a** | **-e** | la cas**a** → le cas**e** |
| **-e** | **-i** | il fior**e** → i fior**i**, la chiav**e** → le chiav**i** |
| accent, one syllable, foreign, short form | no change | la città → le città, il film → i film, la foto → le foto |

## Come in spagnolo

- Masculine *-o*, feminine *-a*: most nouns sit in the same boxes.
- *-ista* words are both genders in both languages: *el / la turista* → **il / la turista**, plural **i turisti / le turiste**.
- Short forms stay short: *la foto, la moto* — and in Italian they don't even change in the plural: **le foto, le moto**.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- *-e* nouns go to **-i**, not *-es*: *los profesores* → **i professori**, *las llaves* → **le chiavi**.
- Italian adds an **h** to keep the hard sound: **amica → amiche**, **collega → colleghe**. (Spanish plays the same spelling trick in verbs: *buscar → busqué*.)
- Words ending in a stressed vowel never change: **un caffè, due caffè** — never *caffès*.

## Occhio!

- Some nouns switch to the feminine in the plural and end in **-a**: **l'uovo → le uova**, **il dito → le dita**, **il braccio → le braccia**, **il labbro → le labbra**. Mostly body parts that come in pairs or sets.
- **l'uomo → gli uomini**, and **la mano → le mani** — feminine, despite the *-o*.
- *-co* words split: **amico → amici** but **gioco → giochi**. Rule of thumb: stressed three syllables from the end (**mè**dico) → **-ci**; otherwise usually **-chi**.

## Curiosità

Why no *-s*? Latin had two plurals: *lupi* for the subject and *lupos* for the object. Spanish built its plurals on *lupos* — hence *lobos* — while Italian took *lupi*. And those odd **le uova, le braccia** are survivors of the Latin neuter plural in *-a* (*ova, bracchia*). Spanish turned some of those plurals into feminine singulars instead: *hoja* comes from *folia*, "leaves" — so every single Spanish leaf carries a Latin plural inside.

## Il trucco

Think of the Italian plural as a vowel swap: **o → i, a → e, e → i**. If the last letter has an accent, or the word is foreign or short, don't touch it.
