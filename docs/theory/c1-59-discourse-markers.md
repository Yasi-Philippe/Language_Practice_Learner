# The little words of spoken Italian

> **allora, insomma, cioè, mica, boh, dai**: they carry almost no information and all of the tone. Learn them and your Italian stops sounding like a textbook.

## La regola

| word | what it does | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| **allora, dunque** | starts, moves on | *bueno, pues* |
| **insomma** | sums up — or loses patience | *en resumen; ¡vamos!* |
| **cioè** | corrects, explains | *o sea* |
| **magari** | maybe; + subjunctive: if only | *a lo mejor; ojalá* |
| **mica** | (not) at all | *nada, ni mucho menos* |
| **pure** | go ahead (after an imperative) | *adelante* |
| **figurati** | don't mention it | *no hay de qué* |
| **boh** | no idea | *ni idea, ¿yo qué sé?* |
| **mah** | doubt | *bueno…, no sé* |
| **dai** | come on | *venga, vamos* |
| **ecco** | there you go | *listo, ahí está* |

## Come in spagnolo

- **cioè** = *o sea* — the filler of a whole generation, in both countries.
- **meno male** = *menos mal*, word for word; **già che ci sei** = *ya que estás*; **neanche per sogno** = *ni en sueños*.
- **secondo me** = *en mi opinión, para mí*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- **mica** strengthens a **non**: **non è mica facile** = *no es nada fácil*. At the start of a sentence it replaces it: **mica male!** = *¡nada mal!*
- **pure** after an imperative isn't "also" but "go ahead": **entra pure** = *adelante, pasa*.
- **dai** isn't "you give" here: **dai, vieni!** = *¡venga, vente!*; **ma dai!** = *¡no me digas!*

## Occhio!

- **figurati** answers thanks, but also means "not at all": **— Ti disturbo? — Figurati!** = *—¿Te molesto? —¡Qué va!*
- **magari** + indicative = maybe (**magari vengo**); **magari** + imperfect subjunctive = if only (**magari vincessi!**).
- **boh** is a shrug in one syllable: fine with friends, not in a job interview.

## Curiosità

**Ciao** began as a declaration of slavery. In old Venetian, **s-ciào vostro** meant "(I am) your slave" — from medieval Latin *sclavus* — the same bowing courtesy as Spanish *servidor de usted*. Goldoni's 18th-century comedies still use it as a polite greeting. Then it shrank into the friendliest word in Italian and travelled: *chao* in much of the Spanish-speaking world, *chau* in Argentina and Uruguay.

## Il trucco

Pair each word with a gesture and it will stick: **boh** with a shrug, **mah** with a tilted hand, **dai** with a beckoning wave, **ecco** with open palms.
