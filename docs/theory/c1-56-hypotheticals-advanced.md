# If… without the "if"

> Real life mixes up its clocks: the mistake was yesterday, the consequence is today. And Italian can drop **se** altogether — a bare subjunctive, a gerund or a tiny **a** does the job.

## La regola

| pattern | example |
|---|---|
| past condition → present result | **se avessi accettato, ora vivrei a Londra** |
| permanent condition → past result | **se fossi più paziente, ieri non avrei litigato** |
| subjunctive alone | **avessi saputo!**, **fossi in te…**, **fosse per me…** |
| **a** + infinitive | **a saperlo, non sarei uscito** |
| gerund | **volendo**, **potendo**, **studiando di più** |
| **se mai** + **dovessi** | **se mai dovessi passare da Roma, chiamami** |
| spoken shortcut | **se lo sapevo, venivo** |

## Come in spagnolo

- Mixed times mix the same way: **se avessi accettato, ora vivrei a Londra** = *si hubiera aceptado, ahora viviría en Londres*.
- **a** + infinitive = *de* + infinitive: **a saperlo** = *de haberlo sabido*.
- The gerund can be a condition in both: **studiando di più, avresti superato l'esame** = *estudiando más, habrías aprobado el examen*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- **fossi in te** = *yo que tú*: Italian steps into your shoes with a subjunctive; Spanish just puts the two pronouns side by side.
- Spoken Italian puts the imperfetto in both halves — **se lo sapevo, venivo** — where colloquial Spanish reaches for the present: *si lo llego a saber, vengo*.
- **se mai dovessi…** adds "should it ever happen": *si alguna vez pasaras por Roma*.

## Occhio!

- **se lo sapevo, venivo** is everyday speech, not a mistake — but in writing go back to **se l'avessi saputo, sarei venuto**.
- A gerund condition keeps one subject for both halves: **studiando di più, avresti superato l'esame** — you study, you pass.

## Curiosità

In *Grammatica della fantasia* (1973), "an introduction to the art of inventing stories", Gianni Rodari — the author on your A1 and A2 shelves — turns hypotheticals into a story machine: take something ordinary, ask **Che cosa succederebbe se…?** — "what would happen if…?" — and let the answer become the plot. Italian teachers still play the game in class.

## Il trucco

Ask "when?" twice: once for the **se** half, once for the result. Each half keeps its own clock.
