# Right now: stare + gerund

> **sto mangiando** = *estoy comiendo*. Same tool — Italians just take it out of the box less often.

## La regola

**stare** (present or imperfetto) + gerund:

| verb ends in | gerund |
|---|---|
| -are | **-ando**: parlando |
| -ere / -ire | **-endo**: prendendo, dormendo |
| fare, dire, bere | **facendo, dicendo, bevendo** |

**stare per** + infinitive = "to be about to": **sto per uscire**.

## Come in spagnolo

- **sto parlando** = *estoy hablando*; **stavo dormendo** = *estaba durmiendo*.
- *-ando* is identical; *-iendo* becomes **-endo**: *durmiendo* → **dormendo**.
- **stare per** = *estar a punto de* — or *estar por* in much of Latin America.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- Italian keeps it for what's happening at this very moment. For longer stretches, the simple present: *estoy viviendo en Roma* → **vivo a Roma**.
- *seguir* + gerund is **continuare a** + infinitive: *sigue lloviendo* → **continua a piovere**.
- *llevar* + gerund is **da** + present: *llevo dos años estudiando* → **studio da due anni**.

## Occhio!

- **essere** has no progressive: *estás siendo injusto* is simply **sei ingiusto**.
- Pronouns go first or stick to the gerund: **lo sto facendo** = **sto facendolo** (the first is more common).

## Curiosità

The gerund's Latin cousin, the gerundive ("which must be…"), lives on as ordinary nouns — in both languages. **La leggenda** (*la leyenda*) is "what must be read", **l'agenda** "what must be done", and **Amanda** "she who must be loved".

## Il trucco

If you could add *ahora mismo* in Spanish, use **stare** + gerund. If not, use the simple present.
