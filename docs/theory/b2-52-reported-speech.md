# He said that… reporting speech

> **«Sono stanco» → Ha detto che era stanco.** Report something in the past and the whole sentence takes a step back — in both languages.

## La regola

| said | reported |
|---|---|
| present: **sono** | imperfetto: **era** |
| passato prossimo: **ho perso** | trapassato: **aveva perso** |
| future: **verrò** | past conditional: **sarebbe venuto** |
| imperative: **chiudi!** | **di** + infinitive: **di chiudere** |
| **qui, oggi, domani, ieri, questo** | **lì, quel giorno, il giorno dopo, il giorno prima, quello** |

## Come in spagnolo

- The same shifts: *dijo que estaba cansado*, *dijo que había perdido las llaves*.
- Time and place words shift too: *aquí → allí*, *mañana → al día siguiente*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- Future → past conditional in Italian (**ha detto che sarebbe venuto**), simple conditional in Spanish (*dijo que vendría*).
- Reported orders: Italian **di** + infinitive (**mi ha detto di chiudere**), Spanish *que* + subjunctive (*me dijo que cerrara*).

## Occhio!

- If the reporting verb is in the present, nothing shifts: **dice che ha fame**.
- Questions become **se** (yes / no) or keep their question word: **mi ha chiesto se volevo…**, **mi ha chiesto dove abitavo**.

## Curiosità

Giovanni Verga built *I Malavoglia* (on your C2 shelf) out of reported speech without the "he said": free indirect speech, where the voices of a whole village slip into the narration. Reading it, you often can't tell who is talking — and that's the point.

## Il trucco

One step back in time for every verb, one step away in space and time for every "here" and "now".
