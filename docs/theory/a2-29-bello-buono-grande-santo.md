# Bel, buon, gran, San: adjectives that shrink

> Four adjectives change shape in front of a noun. Spanish has the same instinct: *buen, gran, San*.

## La regola

- **bello** before a noun behaves like the article: **bel** libro (il), **bello** specchio (lo), **bell'**albero (l'), **bella** casa (la), **bei** libri (i), **begli** occhi (gli), **belle** case (le).
- **buono** behaves like **un**: **buon** viaggio, **buono** studente, **buona** idea (or **buon'idea**).
- **grande** → **gran** before a consonant (optional): **un gran piacere**.
- **santo** → **San** (consonant), **Sant'** (vowel), **Santo** (s + consonant), **Santa** (feminine): **San Pietro, Sant'Antonio, Santo Stefano, Santa Lucia**.

## Come in spagnolo

- **buon** = *buen*, **gran** = *gran*, **San** = *San*: *un buen hombre* = **un buon uomo**.
- **Santo** survives in special cases in both: *Santo Tomás, Santo Domingo* — **Santo Stefano**.
- After the noun nothing shrinks: **un uomo buono** = *un hombre bueno*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- **bello** has seven shapes; Spanish *bello* never changes.
- Spanish greets in the plural (*buenos días, buenas noches*), Italian in the singular: **buongiorno, buonasera, buonanotte**.

## Occhio!

- **begli** is the hardest one: **begli occhi, begli zaini**.
- Masculine **buon** before a vowel has no apostrophe: **buon appetito, buon anno**.

## Curiosità

Italy's oldest nickname is **il Bel Paese**, "the beautiful country". Dante used it in the *Inferno*: "del **bel** paese là dove 'l sì suona" — the beautiful land where *sì* is spoken. Dante really did sort the Romance languages by their word for "yes": *oc*, *oïl* and **sì**.

## Il trucco

For **bello** (and **quello**), say the article first: **il → bel**, **gli → begli**. For **buono**, think of **un**: **un → buon**, **uno → buono**.
