# Tenses that follow the leader

> In Italian the main verb sets the clock for the subordinate one. A present leader takes a present subjunctive; a past leader takes an imperfect one.

## La regola

| leader | same time | earlier | later |
|---|---|---|---|
| present: **penso che** | **sia** | **sia stato** | **sarà** |
| past: **pensavo che** | **fosse** | **fosse stato** | **sarebbe stato** |

## Come in spagnolo

- Spanish follows the same logic: *dudo que sea / dudaba que fuera*, *espero que haya / esperaba que hubiera*.
- **vorrei che** + imperfect = *querría que* + *-ra*: the conditional counts as a past in both.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- The future seen from the past: Spanish simple conditional (*creía que llovería*), Italian past conditional (**credevo che sarebbe piovuto**).
- Italian uses the subjunctive after opinions in every tense, where Spanish often keeps the indicative (*pensaba que estabas…* = **pensavo che fossi…**).

## Occhio!

- Past leader + present subjunctive is the classic slip: *pensavo che sia* ✗ → **pensavo che fosse**.
- Certainty stays indicative in every tense: **sapevo che avevi ragione**.

## Curiosità

Italian schools still teach this under its Latin name, **consecutio temporum** — "the following of the tenses". Spanish grammars call it *correlación de tiempos*. Same rule, inherited from Latin by both.

## Il trucco

Present leader → **sia / abbia**. Past leader → **fosse / avesse**. The future seen from the past → **sarebbe** / **avrebbe** + participle.
