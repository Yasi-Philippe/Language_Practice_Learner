# Can, want, must, know

> Four verbs you'll use in half your sentences — each followed straight by an infinitive, just like in Spanish: **posso venire** = *puedo venir*.

## La regola

| | potere | volere | dovere | sapere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| io | **posso** | **voglio** | **devo** | **so** |
| tu | **puoi** | vuoi | devi | sai |
| lui / lei | **può** | vuole | deve | sa |
| noi | possiamo | vogliamo | **dobbiamo** | **sappiamo** |
| voi | potete | volete | dovete | sapete |
| loro | possono | vogliono | devono | sanno |

## Come in spagnolo

- Modal + infinitive, no preposition: **devo studiare** = *debo estudiar*, **so nuotare** = *sé nadar*.
- **so** and *sé*: both languages turned "I know" into a tiny irregular word.
- **sapere** also means "to taste of": **sa di fragola** = *sabe a fresa*. Same Latin verb, *sapere*: "to have flavor" and "to be wise".
- **sapere** vs **conoscere** is the same split as *saber* vs *conocer*: **so la risposta**, **conosco Marco**.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- **dovere** covers *deber* and *tener que*: **devo andare** = *tengo que irme*.
- **volere** isn't for love. *Te quiero* is **ti voglio bene** (family, friends) or **ti amo** (romance). **Ti voglio** on its own means *te deseo* — use with care.

## Occhio!

- **può** has an accent; **puoi** doesn't.
- **dobbiamo**: the stem changes, *dov-* → **dobb-**.

## Curiosità

Spanish *puede* and Italian **può** went through the same sound change: a stressed Latin *o* split into two vowels — *ue* in Spanish, **uo** in Italian. That's why so many pairs line up: *bueno / **buono***, *nuevo / **nuovo***, *fuego / **fuoco***, *huevo / **uovo***. And Spanish *ie* often matches Italian **ie**: *pie / **piede***, *piedra / **pietra***, *diez / **dieci***.

## Il trucco

See Spanish *ue*? Try Italian **uo**: it works surprisingly often. Not always — Italian only splits the vowel when its syllable ends in a vowel, so *puerta* stays **porta** and *fuerte* stays **forte**.
