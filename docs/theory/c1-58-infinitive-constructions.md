# Infinitives that look back

> **Dopo aver mangiato, siamo usciti.** Spanish says *después de comer*; Italian insists on the past infinitive — and that's only the first of the infinitive's C1 tricks.

## La regola

| construction | example | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| **dopo** + past infinitive | **dopo aver mangiato** | *después de comer* |
| **per** + past infinitive | **grazie per essere venuti** | *gracias por haber venido* |
| **di** + past infinitive (same subject) | **penso di aver capito** | *creo haberlo entendido* |
| **al** + infinitive | **al sorgere del sole** | *al salir el sol* |
| participle on its own | **letti i documenti, firmò** | *leídos los documentos, firmó* |
| **da** + infinitive | **una casa da vendere** | *una casa para vender* |

## Come in spagnolo

- **al** + infinitive = *al* + infinitive: **al sorgere del sole** = *al salir el sol*.
- A participle can open the sentence on its own: **letti i documenti** = *leídos los documentos*; **una volta finita la scuola** = *una vez terminado el colegio*.
- The infinitive can be a noun: **il mangiare bene è un'arte** = *el comer bien es un arte*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- After **dopo**, Italian wants the past infinitive — **dopo aver mangiato**, **dopo essere arrivati** — where Spanish is happy with *después de comer*.
- Each verb brings its own preposition, and it rarely matches: **decidere di partire** (*decidir irse*), **riuscire a finire** (*conseguir terminar*), **smettere di urlare** (*dejar de gritar*).
- Literary Italian glues pronouns onto the participle: **vistolo arrivare** = *al verlo llegar*.

## Occhio!

- **aver** and **esser** often drop the final **-e**, and pronouns stick to the end: **averti**, **averlo**.
- With **essere**, the participle agrees: **dopo essere arrivati**, **grazie per essere venute**.
- **da** + infinitive = "to be done": **una casa da vendere**, **difficile da dire**, **niente da fare**.

## Curiosità

Leopardi's **L'infinito** (1819) — fifteen lines that Italian schoolchildren learn by heart — ends by turning an infinitive into a noun: **«E il naufragar m'è dolce in questo mare»**, "and foundering is sweet to me in this sea". Spanish could do the same: *el naufragar me es dulce*. A poem called *The Infinite* that closes on an infinitive.

## Il trucco

When Spanish says *después de* + infinitive, take one step back in Italian: **dopo aver** / **dopo essere** + participle.
