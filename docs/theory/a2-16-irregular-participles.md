# Participles with personality

> The most used verbs have the most irregular participles — in both languages. The good news: many of them are cousins.

## La regola

| infinitive | participle | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| fare | **fatto** | hecho |
| dire | **detto** | dicho |
| leggere | **letto** | leído |
| scrivere | **scritto** | escrito |
| vedere | **visto** | visto |
| prendere | **preso** | cogido, tomado |
| mettere | **messo** | puesto |
| aprire | **aperto** | abierto |
| chiudere | **chiuso** | cerrado |
| chiedere | **chiesto** | pedido |
| rispondere | **risposto** | respondido |
| vincere | **vinto** | ganado |
| perdere | **perso** | perdido |
| bere | **bevuto** | bebido |

## Come in spagnolo

- **visto** = *visto*, letter for letter. **aperto** ~ *abierto*, **scritto** ~ *escrito*, **morto** ~ *muerto*.
- Spanish has its own irregular gang (*hecho, dicho, puesto, vuelto*), and the Italian ones are often the same words with a different accent.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- Spanish *ch* is often Italian **tt**: *hecho* → **fatto**, *dicho* → **detto**, *noche* → **notte**, *leche* → **latte**, *ocho* → **otto**, *pecho* → **petto**. All come from a Latin *-ct-*: *factum, dictum, noctem, lactem, octo*.
- **preso** (taken) is also a Spanish word: a *preso* is a prisoner — literally "someone taken".

## Occhio!

- **perso** and **perduto** are both correct; **perso** is the everyday one.
- **letto** means "read" and also "bed": **ho letto a letto** = *he leído en la cama*.

## Curiosità

**corso**, the participle of **correre**, is also the word for a main street. **Via del Corso** in Rome got its name from the horse races of the Roman Carnival: riderless horses galloped down it every year, until 1882.

## Il trucco

Say the Spanish participle and "Italianize" it: *ch* → **tt** (*hecho* → **fatto**), *-sto* and *-erto* stay (**visto, aperto**). It works more often than not.
