# Dimmi, fammi, mi dica

> With **tu, noi, voi** the pronouns glue onto the end, as in Spanish (*dímelo*). With the polite **Lei** they jump to the front.

## La regola

- **tu, noi, voi**: pronoun at the end — **leggilo, scrivetemi, andiamoci**.
- **da', di', fa', sta', va'** + pronoun → the consonant doubles: **dammi, dimmi, fammi, stammi, vacci** — except with **gli**: **digli, dagli**.
- Negative **tu**: **non farlo** = **non lo fare**.
- **Lei**: present subjunctive, pronoun in front — **mi dica, si accomodi, venga, prenda**.

## Come in spagnolo

- Pronouns glued to the end: **dimmi** = *dime*, **fallo** = *hazlo*, **scrivetemi** = *escribidme*.
- The polite form borrows the subjunctive in both languages: **venga** = *venga*, **prenda** = *tome*, **scusi** = *perdone*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- With **Lei** the pronoun goes *before* the verb: **mi dica** = *dígame*, **si accomodi** = *siéntese / pase*.
- The doubled consonant: **dammi, fammi** = *dame, hazme*.
- Negative **tu**: **non farlo** — an infinitive, not a subjunctive (*no lo hagas*).

## Occhio!

- **scusa** (to a friend) vs **scusi** (to a stranger): mixing them up is the classic tourist slip.
- **Si accomodi!** is everywhere — doctors, shops, offices: "come in, take a seat".

## Curiosità

Italians address strangers as "she" because **Lei** stood for **Vostra Signoria**, "Your Lordship" — a feminine noun — just as Spanish *usted* comes from *vuestra merced*. In 1938 the Fascist regime campaigned to replace **Lei** with **Voi**, claiming it was a servile import from Spanish. It wasn't, the ban never stuck, and **Lei** won.

## Il trucco

A friend → glue it at the end (**dimmi**). A stranger → put it in front (**mi dica**).
