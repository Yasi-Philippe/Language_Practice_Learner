# Pronouns in pairs: me lo, te la, glielo

> Two pronouns, one beat: **te lo dico**, **glielo do**. Spanish dances the same dance — and even pulls the same trick with "le".

## La regola

| | lo | la | li | le | ne |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mi | me lo | me la | me li | me le | me ne |
| ti | te lo | te la | te li | te le | te ne |
| ci | ce lo | ce la | ce li | ce le | ce ne |
| vi | ve lo | ve la | ve li | ve le | ve ne |
| gli / le / Le | glielo | gliela | glieli | gliele | gliene |
| si | se lo | se la | se li | se le | se ne |

## Come in spagnolo

- Indirect first, direct second: **te lo do** = *te lo doy*.
- Spanish can't say *le lo* and turns it into *se lo*; Italian can't say *gli lo* and turns it into **glielo**.
- Pronouns stick to infinitives and imperatives: **dammelo, devo dirtelo** = *dámelo, tengo que decírtelo*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- **mi, ti, ci, vi, si** become **me, te, ce, ve, se** in front of another pronoun.
- **glie-** works for him, her and polite you, and it's written as one word: **glielo, gliela, gliene**.
- **ne** joins the club too: **me ne, gliene, ce ne** — Spanish has no *ne*.

## Occhio!

- **lo / la** elide before **ho, ha, è**: **gliel'ho dato**, **me l'ha regalata**, **se l'è messa**.
- The participle agrees with the direct pronoun: **me l'ha regalata** (la borsa), **glieli ho portati** (i fiori).

## Curiosità

The Spanish *se* in *se lo doy* isn't reflexive at all. Old Spanish said *gelo doy*, from Latin *illi illum*, "to him it"; *ge* later became *se* and blended with the reflexive. Italian kept the original in view: **glie-lo** still carries **gli**, "to him".

## Il trucco

Turn the first pronoun's vowel into **e** (**mi → me, ti → te, ci → ce**) and add the second. For **gli** and **le**, write **glie-** and glue the rest on.
