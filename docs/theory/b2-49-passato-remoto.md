# The storytelling past

> The tense of history books, novels and fairy tales — and, in the South, of everyday chat. It's the twin of your *indefinido*: **arrivò** = *llegó*.

## La regola

| | parlare | credere | dormire |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | parl**ai** | cred**ei** | dorm**ii** |
| tu | parl**asti** | cred**esti** | dorm**isti** |
| lui / lei | parl**ò** | cred**é** | dorm**ì** |
| noi | parl**ammo** | cred**emmo** | dorm**immo** |
| voi | parl**aste** | cred**este** | dorm**iste** |
| loro | parl**arono** | cred**erono** | dorm**irono** |

Irregular in **io, lui, loro**: **fui, ebbi, feci, dissi, venni, vidi, presi, misi, nacqui, vissi, seppi, scrissi**.

## Come in spagnolo

- The same meaning as the *indefinido*: a finished event in a finished past — **Colombo arrivò nel 1492** = *Colón llegó en 1492*.
- The irregulars look familiar: **fui** = *fui*, **feci** ~ *hice*, **dissi** ~ *dije*, **venni** ~ *vine*, **seppi** ~ *supe*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- In everyday Italian, especially in the North, the passato prossimo takes over: **ieri sono andato** rather than **ieri andai**. In Sicily and much of the South the remoto is still everyday speech.
- Irregular verbs follow a 1-3-3 pattern: irregular in **io, lui, loro** (**feci, fece, fecero**), regular in **tu, noi, voi** (**facesti, facemmo, faceste**).

## Occhio!

- The accent changes everything: **parlò** (he spoke) vs **parlo** (I speak); **dormì** vs **dormi**.
- Many **-ere** verbs have two correct forms: **credei / credetti**, **credé / credette**, **crederono / credettero**.

## Curiosità

**Il fu Mattia Pascal**, Pirandello's novel, uses **fu** — "was" — as an adjective: "the late Mattia Pascal". Italians still say **il fu** before the name of someone who has died. The novel is on your C1 shelf in Books: its hero reads in the paper that he has died — and decides not to correct the mistake.

## Il trucco

Learn the irregulars as a trio — **io, lui, loro**: **feci, fece, fecero**; **dissi, disse, dissero**; **vidi, vide, videro**. The rest of the verb is regular.
