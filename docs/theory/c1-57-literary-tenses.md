# Tenses that live in books

> **Appena ebbe letto la lettera, la bruciò.** Nobody talks like this — but novels do. Spanish has the very same museum piece: *en cuanto hubo leído la carta*.

## La regola

| tense | form | job |
|---|---|---|
| trapassato remoto | **ebbi finito**, **fu partito** | the step just before, after **dopo che, (non) appena, quando** |
| passato remoto | **finì**, **partì** | the events of the story |
| imperfetto | **pioveva**, **era stanco** | the background |

The trapassato remoto = passato remoto of **avere / essere** + participle, and only when the main verb is in the passato remoto: **dopo che ebbero cenato, uscirono**.

## Come in spagnolo

- It's the twin of the *pretérito anterior*: **ebbe letto** = *hubo leído*, **furono arrivati** = *hubieron llegado* — just as rare, just as literary.
- The same story grammar: events in the passato remoto / *indefinido*, background in the imperfect: **quando fu uscito, si accorse che pioveva** = *cuando hubo salido, se dio cuenta de que llovía*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- Verbs of movement and change take **essere**, and the participle agrees: **furono arrivati**, **fu partita**. Spanish always uses *haber*.
- Narration is full of irregular passati remoti that look nothing like Spanish: **scosse** (*negó, sacudió*), **rimase** (*se quedó*), **piansero** (*lloraron*), **presero** (*tomaron*).

## Occhio!

- Outside **dopo che, (non) appena, quando** + passato remoto, the trapassato prossimo does the job: **aveva finito**.
- Many writers skip it altogether: **appena seppe la notizia** is as good as **appena ebbe saputo la notizia**.

## Curiosità

The most famous sentence in Italian is told in the passato remoto: **«Nel mezzo del cammin di nostra vita / mi ritrovai per una selva oscura, / ché la diritta via era smarrita»** — "midway through the journey of our life I found myself in a dark wood, for the straight way was lost". Seven centuries later the recipe hasn't changed: passato remoto for the event (**mi ritrovai**), imperfetto for the background (**era smarrita**) — exactly like *me encontré* and *estaba* in Spanish. Dante is on your C2 shelf.

## Il trucco

**ebbi / fui** + participle = *hube* + participle. If the Spanish sounds like an old book, so does the Italian.
