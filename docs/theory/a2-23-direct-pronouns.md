# Lo, la, li, le: the who and the what

> Direct object pronouns stand in for the person or thing you act on. Spanish speakers have three habits to drop — and one agreement to learn.

## La regola

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **mi** (me) | **ci** (us) |
| 2nd | **ti** (you) | **vi** (you all) |
| 3rd m. | **lo** (him, it) | **li** (them) |
| 3rd f. | **la** (her, it) · **La** (you, polite) | **le** (them) |

Before **ho, hai, ha, hanno**: **lo / la** → **l'**. In the passato prossimo the participle agrees: **l'ho visto / vista, li ho visti, le ho viste**.

## Come in spagnolo

- Same position — before the verb, or glued to an infinitive: **lo vedo** = *lo veo*, **devo vederlo** = *tengo que verlo*.
- **lo so / non lo so** = *lo sé / no lo sé*.
- Repeating the topic is fine in both: **il caffè lo prendo senza zucchero** = *el café lo tomo sin azúcar*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- No personal *a*: *veo **a** Marco* → **vedo Marco**. Never *vedo a Marco*.
- Masculine plural is **li**, not *los*; feminine plural is **le**, not *las*.
- No *leísmo*: *le vi ayer* → **l'ho visto ieri** — always **lo** for a man.

## Occhio!

- **li** and **le** never take an apostrophe: **li ho comprati**, **le ho viste**.
- The agreement is compulsory after **lo, la, li, le**: **Le chiavi? Le ho dimenticate.** Spanish never agrees here (*las he olvidado*).

## Curiosità

In Spain, *le veo* for "I see him" — the famous *leísmo* — is accepted by the Real Academia when it refers to a man. Italian has no such debate: **gli** and **le** are strictly "to him" and "to her", and you'll never hear *gli vedo*. One argument fewer at the dinner table.

## Il trucco

Delete the Spanish *a* before people and swap the plurals: *los / las* → **li / le**. Then make the participle agree.
