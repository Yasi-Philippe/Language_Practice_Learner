# Giving orders, Italian style

> The informal imperative is short, loud and mostly familiar — with one big gift: the negative is easier than in Spanish.

## La regola

| | parlare | prendere | sentire | finire |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tu | **parla!** | **prendi!** | **senti!** | **finisci!** |
| noi | **parliamo!** | **prendiamo!** | **sentiamo!** | **finiamo!** |
| voi | **parlate!** | **prendete!** | **sentite!** | **finite!** |
| non + tu | **non parlare!** | **non prendere!** | **non sentire!** | **non finire!** |

Short **tu** forms: **va', fa', da', sta', di'** (or **vai, fai, dai, stai**) · **sii** (be) · **abbi** (have).

## Come in spagnolo

- **parla!** = *¡habla!*, **aspetta!** = *¡espera!*.
- **di' la verità!** = *¡di la verdad!* — the very same word.
- **abbi pazienza** = *ten paciencia*; **sii buono** = *sé bueno*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- *-ere* and *-ire* verbs end in **-i** for **tu**: *¡abre!* → **apri!**, *¡toma!* → **prendi!**.
- **noi** and **voi** are simply the present: **andiamo!** (*¡vamos!*), **parlate!** (*¡hablad!*).
- Negative **tu** is **non** + infinitive: **non parlare!** — no subjunctive needed (*¡no hables!*).

## Occhio!

- Negative **voi** is the ordinary form: **non aprite!** = *¡no abráis!*
- **va'** and **vai** are both right — the apostrophe marks a missing **i**.

## Curiosità

The most Italian word of all, **Dai!** ("come on!"), is simply the imperative of **dare**, "give". Spain does the same with another verb: *¡venga!* Two languages, two different imperatives, one meaning — *let's go, hurry up, you can do it*.

## Il trucco

Want to say "don't" to a friend? Put **non** before the infinitive and you're done: **non mangiare, non correre, non dimenticare**.
