# Che, cui, chi: linking sentences

> Spanish juggles *que, quien, el que, el cual, cuyo*. Italian gets by with fewer: **che** most of the time, **cui** after a preposition.

## La regola

| use | Italian | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| subject or direct object | **che** | *que* |
| after a preposition | **a cui, con cui, di cui, in cui, per cui** | *al que, con quien, del que…* |
| whose | **il cui, la cui, i cui, le cui** | *cuyo, cuya…* |
| formal alternative | **il quale, la quale, i quali, le quali** | *el cual, la cual…* |
| whoever | **chi** | *quien, el que* |
| what (the thing that) | **quello che, ciò che** | *lo que* |

## Come in spagnolo

- **che** = *que*, for people and things: **il ragazzo che parla, il libro che leggo**.
- **il quale** = *el cual*: same formal flavour, same agreement.
- **il cui** agrees with what is owned, like *cuyo*: **la ragazza il cui padre…** = *la chica cuyo padre…*

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- After a preposition it's **cui**, never *che*: **la persona con cui lavoro** (not *con che*).
- No article before **cui**: **con cui** = *con el que / con quien*.
- *Lo que* is **quello che / ciò che** — not *lo che*.

## Occhio!

- **il giorno in cui** = *el día (en) que*: Italian needs the **in**.
- **chi** = the one who: **chi dorme non piglia pesci**. It's not a question here.

## Curiosità

**cui** and *cuyo* are siblings. Latin *qui* had a whole family: *cuius*, "whose", became Spanish *cuyo*; *cui*, "to whom", became Italian **cui**. The same family — each language kept a different member.

## Il trucco

Preposition in front? **cui**. No preposition? **che**. Spanish *lo que*? **quello che**.
