# Gli or le? Spanish "le" splits in two

> Indirect pronouns answer *to whom?* Spanish has one *le*; Italian chooses by gender.

## La regola

| to… | pronoun | example |
|---|---|---|
| me | **mi** | **mi dai il numero?** |
| you | **ti** | **ti scrivo domani** |
| him | **gli** | **gli regalo un libro** |
| her | **le** | **le regalo dei fiori** |
| you (Lei) | **Le** | **Le presento mia moglie** |
| us | **ci** | **ci spiega la lezione** |
| you all | **vi** | **vi porto un regalo** |
| them | **gli** (or **loro** after the verb) | **gli do l'acqua** · **do loro l'acqua** |

## Come in spagnolo

- Same slot before the verb: **gli scrivo** = *le escribo*.
- Same verbs: **dare, dire, scrivere, regalare, chiedere a qualcuno** = *dar, decir, escribir, regalar, preguntar a alguien*.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- *le* → **gli** (him) or **le** (her); *les* → **gli**.
- Some verbs switch type. **telefonare a** is indirect (**le telefono**) where Spanish *llamar* is direct (*la llamo*). **ringraziare** is direct (**La ringrazio**) where *agradecer* is indirect (*le agradezco*).
- No doubling: *le doy el libro a María* → **do il libro a Maria** or **le do il libro** — not both.

## Occhio!

- **le** is a trap word: **le telefono** (I call her) vs **le vedo** (I see them, feminine).
- **gli** for "to them" is everyday Italian; **loro** after the verb is the formal option.

## Curiosità

Both languages hated the sequence "to-him it". Spanish fixed *le lo* by turning it into *se lo* (*se lo doy*); Italian fused it into **glielo** (**glielo do**). Two different patches for the same itch — you'll meet **glielo** at B1.

## Il trucco

Spanish *le*? Ask "a man or a woman?" Man → **gli**, woman → **le**. More than one person → **gli**.
