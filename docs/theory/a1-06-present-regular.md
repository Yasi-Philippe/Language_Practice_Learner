# Three families, and no "-s" for "you"

> **-are, -ere, -ire**: the same three families as Spanish *-ar, -er, -ir*, with endings you'll half recognize.

## La regola

| | parlare | prendere | dormire | capire |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| io | parl**o** | prend**o** | dorm**o** | cap**isco** |
| tu | parl**i** | prend**i** | dorm**i** | cap**isci** |
| lui / lei | parl**a** | prend**e** | dorm**e** | cap**isce** |
| noi | parl**iamo** | prend**iamo** | dorm**iamo** | cap**iamo** |
| voi | parl**ate** | prend**ete** | dorm**ite** | cap**ite** |
| loro | parl**ano** | prend**ono** | dorm**ono** | cap**iscono** |

## Come in spagnolo

- The skeleton is the same: **parlo / hablo, parla / habla, parlate / habláis**.
- Spelling protects the sound in both languages: *buscar → busqué*, **cercare → cerchi, cerchiamo**; *pagar → pagué*, **pagare → paghi**.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- **tu** always ends in **-i**: **parli, prendi, dormi** — no *-s* (Italian lost the Latin final *-s*, the same reason its plurals have none).
- **noi** always ends in **-iamo**, whatever the family: **parliamo, prendiamo, dormiamo** (*hablamos, tomamos, dormimos*).
- **-ere** and **-ire** share **-ono** in the plural: **prendono, dormono**.

## Occhio!

- Stress! The loro forms are stressed three syllables from the end: **pàrlano, àbitano, tèlefonano**. Spanish ears want *parlàno* — resist.
- **-iare** verbs keep only one **i**: **studiare → tu studi, noi studiamo**.

## Curiosità

That strange **-isc-** in **capisco, finisco, preferisco** is an old Latin suffix, *-sc-*, that meant "starting to". Spanish has it too, just elsewhere: in the *-ecer* verbs (*florecer, oscurecer, crecer*) and in the *-zco* of *conozco* and *parezco*. Italian **fiorire → fiorisco** and Spanish *florecer* both come from Latin *florescere*.

## Il trucco

For the **-isc-** verbs, **noi** and **voi** never get the **-isc-**: **capiamo, capite**. Everyone else does.
