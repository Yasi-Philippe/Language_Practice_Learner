# Small words, big traps

> Prepositions are where Spanish speakers trip most — not because they're hard, but because they look *almost* the same.

## La regola

| Italian | mostly means | example |
|---|---|---|
| **di** | of, from (origin) | **sono di Madrid** |
| **a** | to, at — cities | **vado a Roma** |
| **da** | from, at someone's place, since | **vengo da Napoli**, **vado da Marco**, **da tre anni** |
| **in** | in, to — countries, rooms; by — transport | **in Spagna**, **in cucina**, **in treno** |
| **con** | with | **con te** |
| **su** | on, about | **un libro su Roma** |
| **per** | for, through | **per te**, **per strada** |
| **tra / fra** | between; in (time) | **tra due giorni** |

## Come in spagnolo

- **a piedi, a cavallo** = *a pie, a caballo*.
- **di** for possession: **la macchina di Paolo** = *el coche de Paolo*.
- **con** = *con*, and **su** = *sobre* (on, about).

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- Cities take **a**, countries take **in** — both for going and for living: **vado a Roma, vado in Italia**, **abito a Roma, abito in Italia**. Spanish: *voy a Roma, voy a Italia*, *vivo en Roma, vivo en Italia*.
- **per** = *para* AND *por*: **un regalo per te**, **grazie per tutto**, **passo per Milano**. One word instead of two — enjoy it.
- **tra due giorni** = *dentro de dos días*. **In due giorni** means *en dos días* — "it took two days".

## Occhio!

- **da** is not *de*. It means *from* a place (**vengo da Roma**), *at someone's house or shop* (**da Marco, dal medico**) and *since / for* with things still going on (**studio italiano da tre anni** = *desde hace tres años*).
- **sono di Roma** (I'm from Rome — origin) but **vengo da Roma** (I'm coming from Rome — journey).

## Curiosità

**Leonardo da Vinci** isn't a surname: it means "Leonardo from Vinci", a small town near Florence. And Italians choose between **tra** and **fra** by ear, to dodge tongue-twisters: **fra tre giorni** (not *tra tre*), **tra fratelli** (not *fra fratelli*).

## Il trucco

Where Spanish says *a casa de* or *desde hace*, Italian says **da**. Where Spanish hesitates between *por* and *para*, Italian just says **per**.
