# Piacere: a home game for Spanish speakers

> **Mi piace** works exactly like *me gusta*: the thing you like is the subject. English speakers suffer here — you don't.

## La regola

| to… | + one thing or a verb | + plural |
|---|---|---|
| me | **mi piace** | **mi piacciono** |
| you | **ti piace** | **ti piacciono** |
| him | **gli piace** | **gli piacciono** |
| her / you (Lei) | **le piace** | **le piacciono** |
| us | **ci piace** | **ci piacciono** |
| you all | **vi piace** | **vi piacciono** |
| them | **gli piace** (or **piace a loro**) | **gli piacciono** |

## Come in spagnolo

- **mi piace la pizza** = *me gusta la pizza*; **mi piacciono i gatti** = *me gustan los gatos*.
- Verbs count as one thing: **mi piace ballare e cantare** = *me gusta bailar y cantar*.
- **mi piaci** = *me gustas* — "I like you". Handle with care.

## Diverso dallo spagnolo

- Spanish *le* splits in two: **gli** (to him) and **le** (to her).
- No doubled pronoun: *a Marco le gusta* → **a Marco piace**. Italian says it once.

## Occhio!

- **a me mi piace** is the most famous "mistake" in Italian schools: teachers circle it in red, because **a me** and **mi** say the same thing twice. Your Spanish *a mí me gusta* is perfectly correct — and in relaxed speech, Italians say it anyway.
- In the past, **piacere** takes **essere**: **mi è piaciuto il film** (you'll meet it at A2).

## Curiosità

**Piacere!** is also how Italians say *¡Encantado!* when they meet you, and **per piacere** is a twin of **per favore**. Same word as Spanish *placer* — which is why **con piacere** = *con mucho gusto*.

## Il trucco

Ask "what is doing the pleasing?" One thing or an action → **piace**. Several things → **piacciono**.
